The video is framed as a dialogue with Laozi and uses the Daodejing’s line “The Way of Heaven reduces the surplus to supplement the deficient; the Way of Man does the opposite, taking from the deficient to serve the surplus” as its thesis. It explains the operating rules behind the wealth gap and offers a methodology for ordinary people to break through.

I. The Core Proposition: One Sentence That Reveals the Law of the World

The Way of Heaven reduces the surplus to supplement the deficient; The Way of Man is different: it takes from the deficient to serve the surplus. — Daodejing

This sentence reveals two layers of rules:

The Way of Heaven (natural law)The Way of Man (social law)
DirectionReduce the surplus, supplement the deficient (take from the rich to help the poor; maintain balance)Take from the deficient, serve the surplus (take from the poor to enrich the rich; the strong get stronger)
GoalSeek balance; no single power may dominateReward the strong; devour the weak
AnalogyEagles breed slowly, sparrows breed quickly; the top is cappedSnowballing; the Matthew Effect

A natural person asks: Why does the Way of Heaven support the weak, while the Way of Man robs the poor to enrich the rich?

II. Why Does the Way of Heaven “Cut Peaks and Fill Valleys”? — Nature’s Invisible Hand

  • Use ecological niches as an analogy: a sparrow lays five or six eggs per clutch and has two or three clutches a year; an eagle lays only one or two eggs a year, and its chicks have a high mortality rate.
  • If eagles also bred wildly and all their chicks survived, they would eat all their prey and eventually go extinct themselves.
  • → This is “reducing the surplus to supplement the deficient”: nature firmly caps the reproductive lifeline of apex predators in order to preserve life at the bottom.
  • Conclusion: The Way of Heaven does not allow one power to dominate or a winner to take all.

III. Why Does the Way of Man Work in Reverse? — “Social Consensus” and the Matthew Effect

The Way of Man works in the opposite direction because human society has built a system of “social consensus”. The modern scholar Robert K. Merton coined the term “Matthew Effect” for the same phenomenon, and it is the core of the Way of Man:

For to everyone who has, more will be given; but from the one who has not, even what he has will be taken away.

An academic example: the same paper —

  • with a giant’s name attached → causes a sensation;
  • with an unknown author’s name attached → gathers dust in a pile of papers.
  • Fame automatically mints more fame; capital automatically breeds more capital.

A coffee shop example: two coffee shops open on the same street at the same time —

  • the one on the left hangs a well-known chain’s sign: headquarters handles site selection and the supply chain, and people come on day one because of the brand;
  • the one on the right is run by a young independent owner. The coffee is just as good, but few people walk in during the first month.

The reason: the left one has endorsement and backing, so customers do not have to take a risk; the right one may be better, but nobody knows about it.

The cruelty of the Way of Man is this: it only looks at whether you have initial chips in your hand. For the same mistake, someone with chips gets a scratch; someone without chips is out of the game. The gap widens bit by bit. In the end it looks like a gap in ability, but from the very beginning it was a gap in chips. “The system itself rewards the strong and devours the weak.”

IV. Can the Rules of the Way of Man Be Broken? — You Cannot Escape the Circle Drawn by the Way of Heaven

Man follows the earth, the earth follows heaven, heaven follows the Way, and the Way follows nature.

  • No matter how much the Way of Man jumps around, it cannot escape the circle drawn by the Way of Heaven.
  • When something that once flourished reaches a critical point, the balancing mechanism of the Way of Heaven kicks in: the too-strong are weakened, the too-concentrated are dispersed.
  • But the key is: that critical point will not come looking for you. You have to create the conditions yourself and make it tilt in your direction.

A Methodology for Breaking Through (Step by Step)

Step 1 | Let go of emotions

  • Many people, once they see the nature of “the rich getting richer”, become cynical. But emotion is the cheapest thing there is; it traps you in a dead end.
  • Analogy: when a strong wind hits at sea, cursing the sky will not move the boat. A real sailor shuts up immediately, watches the wind, and adjusts the sails.
  • → Acknowledge that the system exists, treat it as objective terrain, and then look for leverage.

Step 2 | First “have” a snowball, rather than “roll” one

  • The Way of Man is like snowballing: if you already have a snowball, one push makes it bigger; if you do not, the most important thing is not to find a steep slope but to first pack a ball of snow.
  • The essence of being “deficient” = not yet having an initial snowball (no resources, no endorsement).
  • → The urgent question is not “how to roll” but “how to first have something”.

Step 3 | Let people see you first

  • You have done a lot of work but nobody sees it? That is the biggest problem.
  • You must first make people notice you; only then will they come near you and give you resources.
  • Do not believe that “gold will always shine”. If you do not show it, nobody knows what you are made of. Let people see you first.

Step 4 | One word: “good” — be patient and accumulate

  • Most people do not lose at the starting line; they lose to impatience: they act blindly when provoked and lie flat when frustrated.
  • People who truly gain a foothold are those who quietly accumulate strength for a long time in a corner where nobody is watching.
  • The bamboo analogy: it can shoot up because its roots have accumulated nutrients across hundreds of square meters underground. The roots go deep.

Step 5 | The highest good is like water: work with a high profile, live with a low profile

  • After accumulating resources, you must remember another line: the highest good is like water — water nourishes all things yet always stays in the lowest place.
  • If you show your edge and sit high above others, the Way of Heaven will immediately suppress you. A good line: work with a high profile, but live with a low profile.
  • Showing is necessary, but “what to show, how much to show, and when to show” matters:

    Let others see that you are doing things, but do not let them see through what you are thinking. Put your abilities on the table; keep your thoughts in your belly.

V. For Managers: Plant Seeds of the Way of Heaven Within the Framework of the Way of Man

  • Teams also follow the Way of Man: high performers get more resources, while average performers are gradually pushed out.
  • If left alone, the team will inevitably end up with a few people holding all the opportunities while the rest coast, and efficiency will decline.
  • A good manager must plant seeds of the Way of Heaven within the framework of the Way of Man:
    • when a big tree blocks all the sunlight, as the person who sets the rules, you must cut away the excess branches;
    • bind the interests of the strong to the growth of newcomers;
    • assign people who compete for credit to different projects so they compete separately;
    • when the system is about to monopolize, you must be the “heaven” that starts “reducing the surplus to supplement the deficient”.

VI. Conclusion: Understand and Use Both the Way of Heaven and the Way of Man

The Way of Heaven gives me awe and balance; the Way of Man forces me to show and accumulate. A low starting point is not frightening. What is frightening is being kidnapped by anger and refusing to think about how to make the rules work for you. The Way of Heaven and the Way of Man are not a choice between two; you must understand and use both at the same time — the Way of Heaven gives you the bottom line and the direction; the Way of Man gives you the methods and the path.

If you want to change, the best time is to start right now.
